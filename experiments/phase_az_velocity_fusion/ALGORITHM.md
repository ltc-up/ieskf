The primary new information is **dimension- and source-qualified velocity**, not a new Kalman gain formula. Original NDT100, map0105, rectangular source crop, true CUDA Deskew, IMU/Q, FitnessR, full15D and delayed repropagation remain unchanged. ## Changes and hypotheses

1. **X ablation**: AY raw opticalXY becomes sensor-x only, with its original100ms fallback. This isolates the consequence of adding a lateral component that the current production planar EKF does not directly consume. It is not an assertion that opticalY is always wrong. Full15D Jacobian/cross-covariance preserved. No vy/vz pseudo-measurement.
2. **M managed forward source**: same scalar sensor-x model plus source lifecycle. INIT/RECOVERING, ACTIVE, FAULT, STALE and LOW_SPEED_WHEEL are distinct. Wheel is fused only when optical is not active; no independent double-forward information. Bad optical immediately leaves ACTIVE, rather than AY retaining its last accepted sample's100ms eligibility. Silent missing data expire after100ms. Recovery requires100ms consecutive fresh qualified samples. This borrows FAR-LIO's explicit validity/freshness/reentry pattern, not its5/9-state dynamics or residual clipping.
3. **S managed planar speed**: tests whether useful optical speed can be retained without directly trusting the measured direction. Let two-row sensor projection A include actual installed rotation, r be sensor-minus-IMU lever and u=R.T v+(omega-bg)×r. Observation `z=norm(velocity_raw_xy)`, `h=norm(Au)`. If B is the original2x15 optical Jacobian, `H=(Au).T/norm(Au) B`. Full15D Joseph/right-SO3 reset inherited. No body-vy=0, sensor-z=0, artificial gain or prior inflation. It measures speed in the actual observed sensor plane, **not an invented full3D norm**. Near zero this scalar loses observability, so wheel supplies signed forward speed.

S's variance is `n_z.T R_xy n_z`, retaining any supplied off-diagonal2D covariance before projection. With unchanged AY isotropic engineeringR this remains0.26(m/s)^2. First-order propagation and nonzero-speed validity are explicit; it is not a statistical calibration. The norm has nonlinear noise bias, small only under the declared high-SNR/high-speed approximation. Its heading invariance and full15D Jacobian are numerically tested. It cannot directly estimate sideslip direction, so lost corner benefit is a falsifiable downside, not an implementation surprise.

## Lifecycle and anomaly evidence

- Optical qualified status is the same AY past-arrived stst0/age/frame/finite rule; unknown manufacturer bit semantics are not invented. Optical corrected/internal-IMU output is never fused alongside raw.
- Low-speed threshold1m/s comes from actual production switching. Existing5m/s motion mismatch envelope is reused for dynamic-jump rejection and wheel-versus-optical-zero conflict, not tuned by GNSS. A change in raw speed minus the corresponding change in predicted sensor speed exceeding5m/s is a **gross innovation-change fault**. It is not a calibrated NIS test or proof that every slower bias is detectable.
-100ms timeout and recovery are inherited AY freshness scale/ten100Hz measurement periods. They are explicitly engineering choices; no manufacturer guarantee is asserted. Recovery initially relies on wheel; no fabricated covariance decay or arbitrary velocity blend.
- Both sources retain their real Header and reference point. No past wheel speed is relabelled as a simultaneous optical observation. Original bounded500ms checkpoint replay carries lifecycle metadata across IMU, NDT and delayed insertions. Each real observation is assimilated once per reconstructed history. Source exceptions never reset means/bias/P or delete cross terms.
- Sensor residual disagreement marks a risk. With no independently calibrated cross-correlation, radius, slip or delay model, AZ **selects** overlapping longitudinal likelihoods rather than pretending they are independent. No covariance intersection claim is made. Temporal sample dependence within each source and shared model/map errors remain unresolved; first packet per10ms bin does not make data independent.

## Why these routes, not a fitted reliability curve?

Actual raw-wheel/optical disagreement is much stronger in braking than acceleration and differs with turning. That supports an anomaly diagnostic, but cannot uniquely assign fault to wheel slip: Header/record age, frontend filtering, tyre geometry and optical error also contribute. The fixed sensor-only lag scan has no unique independent time reference; no fitted lag is applied. A continuous weight derived from GNSS error or specific corner would overfit this benchmark. Adding two forward observations as independent would manufacture confidence. A learned bias/scale could absorb uncalibrated installation/time/map error and is not introduced without an identifiable physical experiment.

## Validation versus deployment

Both M and S are genuine own-state CUDA-NDT candidates with1468-frame and4134-frame continuous runs. X is a single-factor1468 ablation. S fault injection tests recovery under missing2s and +15m/s optical corruption with valid status; inputs/offsets frozen before the fault run. Tests of bad status, timeout, reentry, duplicate/late events, raw speed rotation invariance and full analytic H supplement actual data. They do not certify physical fault coverage. No Q/R search, geography-based policy, production write or default change.
